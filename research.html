WHAT HAPPENS AFTER YOU PRESS ENTER?

The Journy Behind
Every Prompt

Every AI response begins a journey.

Invisible to us, visible in impact

1.You type a prompt

Every AI interaction begins with a user prompt. Although the process appears instantaneous, it triggers a series of physical operations across computing infrastructure.

2.It travels across the internet

Your prompt is transmitted through network infrastructure to the servers that process AI requests. Within seconds, it reaches a data center where computation begins.

3.It reaches a data center

AI models run inside large data centers containing thousands of servers. These facilities require reliable access to electricity, cooling systems, and network infrastructure.

4.Electricity powers the response

AI responses are generated through large-scale computation. GPUs and supporting hardware consume electricity throughout the inference process.

5.Heat is generated and water keeps systems cool

Intensive computation produces large amounts of heat.
Without cooling, AI servers cannot operate reliably.
Computation generates heat that must be removed to maintain safe operating temperatures. Many data centers rely on water-based cooling systems, making freshwater an important operational resource.

6.Carbon is emitted

Electricity generation can result in greenhouse gas emissions. The carbon footprint of an AI response depends on the energy sources supplying the data center.

7.Communities experience the impact

Data centers can increase local demand for water, electricity, and land. In some communities, this can put pressure on water supplies, utility infrastructure, electricity costs, and surrounding neighborhoods.

Three Invisible Resources

Water

0mL*

≈ 2 cups, per visitor interaction

Water is used in data centers to cool servers and remove heat. The amount varies by location, climate and cooling technology. The 500 mL figure is the installation's anchor — roughly 50 prompts, scaled to a volume you can see in the tank.

Electricity

0Wh*

≈ 1.5 phone charges, per visitor interaction

More computation means more energy. Model size, response length, and hardware efficiency all affect electricity use.

Carbon

~0.0g CO2*

≈ driving ~200 m, highly dependent on energy source

The same computation can create different emissions depending on the local electricity mix.

* est. v1.3 · IL-grounded · Li et al. 2025 · EPA eGRID2023
location-based · offsets excluded · 500mL/50-prompt anchor. Actual impact depends on location and energy mix.

Illustrative estimates based on published studies. Actual impact varies by model, prompt and response length, hardware, data-center location, cooling system, and electricity mix.

US Data Behind the Numbers

Training Cost

0L

the biggest cost already happened

On-site freshwater for GPT-3 (175B) training. 5.4 million litres total across US data centers. A medium request today is the equivalent of 10–50 requests per 500 mL bottle of water, depending on location. (US grid average: 3.14 L consumed, 43.8 L withdrawn, per kWh — WRI.)

By Location

0–0mL

same request, different states

US average 16.9 mL. Illinois 15.0 mL. Georgia 10.6 mL. Arizona 29.9 mL. Washington 47.5 mL. Water intensity varies with local electricity grid mix and data center cooling design.

At Scale

0TWh

and growing fast

US data-center electricity use in 2023 (4.4% of national total). Up from 76 TWh (1.9%) in 2018, projected to reach 325–580 TWh (6.7–12.0%) by 2028. Direct water consumption more than tripled, from 21.2 to 66 billion litres, between 2014 and 2023 — 84% of it now at hyperscale and colocation facilities.

Sources: Li et al. (2023); Shehabi et al. (2024, LBNL).

For comparison: one AI query (~4.3 g CO2) sits above a minute of web browsing (0.1–0.2 g) or an email (0.02–0.17 g), close to a minute of streaming video (0.6–1.7 g), and far below driving one mile (~404 g) or a 10-minute hot shower (~2,000 g). (NIAIS brief.)
